Convolution: flip, slide, measure the overlap

What ∫ x(τ) h(t−τ) dτ is actually doing — EE341 §3.2

Convolution is: flip one signal, slide it, and measure how much the two overlap at each position.

The integral computes one number per slide position. Collect those numbers as you slide, and you have y(t).

1. Why there is a flip at all

The −τ in h(t−τ) does two things at once, and it helps to separate them:

Left: h(τ) as given. Middle: h(−τ) — mirrored about τ = 0. Right: h(t−τ) — the mirrored copy, slid right by t (the arrow marks t). Drag the slider in §2 and it is the right-hand shape that moves.
A deliberately lopsided shape is used here: if h is symmetric — like rect — the mirroring is invisible, which is exactly why the flip is so easy to forget.

Where the flip comes from. Recall the meaning from §3.1: h(t−τ) is "how much is the poke from time τ still ringing at time t", and t−τ is elapsed time. As τ runs forward through the pokes, the elapsed time runs backward. That reversal is the flip. It is not a trick — it is what "how long ago" means.

3. Read your worked problem off the picture

Select rect for both, then step through these four positions:

tWhat you seeWhy
t = −1.5no overlap, y = 0the sliding window is entirely left of x
t = −0.5half overlap, y = 0.5edges just half-covering; y = 1 + t
t = 0full overlap, y = 1the two rects sit exactly on top of each other — the peak
t = +0.7shrinking, y = 0.3y = 1 − t as it slides out

Trace the dot across the whole slide and it draws a triangle. That is the result:

rect(t) * rect(t) = tri(t)

The two cases in your notes are the two sides of the triangle. Sliding in from the left (t < 0), overlap grows: 1 + t. Sliding out to the right (t > 0), overlap shrinks: 1 − t. The split is at t = 0 because that is where the window stops entering and starts leaving. Together: 1 − |t|.

The case-splitting rule, stated generally. You are intersecting two intervals, so the limits are

lower = max(all lower limits)     upper = min(all upper limits)

A new case begins at each t where the winner of a max or a min changes. For two equal-width rects that happens once (at t = 0) → two cases. For unequal widths it happens twice → three cases, the middle one being a flat top where the narrow signal sits entirely inside the wide one. Try rect against wide rect above and watch the plateau appear.

4. Things the picture makes obvious

  • Widths add. Overlap starts the instant the leading edges touch and ends when the trailing edges part. Watch the support of y — it is always as wide as the two supports combined.
  • Commutativity. Swap x and h in the pickers. The output is identical. Sliding x past h or h past x measures the same overlap.
  • Convolution smooths. Two hard-edged rects give a continuous triangle. Convolving with anything of finite width is a weighted local average, which is why filters blur sharp edges — and why a bandlimited channel spreads a pulse into its neighbours (that is intersymbol interference, later in the course).
  • Convolving with δ does nothing. Pick impulse for h: the sliding "signal" is a single spike, so the overlap integral just samples x at that point and y is a copy of x. That is x * δ = x, seen rather than proved.
