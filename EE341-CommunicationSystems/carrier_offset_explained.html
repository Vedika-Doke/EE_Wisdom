Frequency offset = a slowly rotating phasor

Worked problem 9.1 and your notes page 11, seen in the complex plane — EE341

The complex envelope is the signal seen from a reference frame spinning at fc.

Multiplying by e−j2πfct literally means "spin the coordinate system at the carrier rate". Get the rate exactly right and the phasor freezes. Get it slightly wrong by Δf, and it creeps around at Δf — that residual creep is the frequency offset.

3. Why the envelope beats

In the page-11 case the complex envelope is a sum of two vectors:

xℓ(t) = A·x(t)  +  A·ej2πΔf t
  • A·x(t) — the message. Real, so it lies flat along the horizontal axis, sliding back and forth (and going negative).
  • A·ej2πΔf t — the injected carrier with its offset. Constant length, rotating at Δf.

The envelope the detector reports is the length of the sum. Because the second vector keeps changing direction, sometimes it lines up with the message and sometimes it opposes it:

A(t) = A·√( x²(t) + 2·x(t)·cos(2πΔf t) + 1 )
Δfwhat happens
Δf = 0 (locked)the rotating vector stands still at angle 0, so it simply adds 1 to the message: A(t) = A·|x(t) + 1|. Clean DSB-AM. This is why injecting a carrier lets a cheap envelope detector work.
Δf ≠ 0the vector wanders around, alternately reinforcing and cancelling the message. The envelope fades in and out at rate Δf — the beating you hear on a mistuned receiver.

Watch for the phase flip. Set Δf small but nonzero and let it run. Twice per beat cycle the rotating vector passes through the negative real direction; if the message is small there, the sum sweeps close to the origin and the envelope collapses. That near-origin pass is where the demodulated output goes to mush — it is the same "envelope through zero" failure as over-modulation, arriving by a different route.

4. What to take away

  • Phase offset → phasor parked at a fixed angle. Frequency offset → phasor rotating at that rate. Set φ₀ with Δf = 0 and compare.
  • Complex envelopes add when bandpass signals add (Re{} is linear), which is the entire trick on page 11 — no trigonometric identities required.
  • A PLL's job, in one sentence: stop the rotation and drive the angle to zero.
  • The envelope never cares about absolute phase — only length. That is exactly why an envelope detector is immune to φ₀ but ruined by Δf.
